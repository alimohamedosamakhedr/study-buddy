Modal verbs

May can be used for polite request, formal permission or level of certinty

Might can used for level of certinty and polite request

Should can be used when giving advice or 90% level of certainty

Ought to can be used when giving advice

Had better is used when giving advice but with threat of a bad result

Be supposed to is used when there is an expectation or an unfulfilled expectation

Must is used in strong necessity, prohibiton or high level of certinty

Have to is used in necessity or lack of it

Have got to is used in necessity

Will is used in complete certainty,willingess or polite request

Modifier

A modifier can be a word (adjectives, adverbs, demonstratives, possessive determiners, degree modifiers, intensifiers), a phrase (Prepositional phrases), or even a clause that serves as an adjective or an adverb to provide additional information about another word or group of words. Modifiers could either be restrictive (essential to the meaning of a sentence) or non-restrictive (additional/ non-essential elements in a sentence).

Misplaced Modifiers

A modifier ought to be placed beside the words they modify, and misplaced modifiers can cause the readers to misinterpret the intended meaning.

Correcting Misplaced Modifiers

To correct the misplaced modifier issue, we should place the modifiers right before or after the word they modify

Dangling Modifiers

`

Dangling modifiers refer to descriptive words that are used to start a sentence but do not really describe what the writer intended them to describe. It is important to remember that a modifier that starts a sentence must be followed by the word it is supposed to describe, immediately.
